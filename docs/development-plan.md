# Development plan

## Phase 0 — clarify the job

Define users, installation environments, tile pitch, acceptable visual language, removal frequency, and what “tool-free” means. Interview at least five likely users and collect examples of existing backsplash pain points.

**Deliverable:** approved product brief and decision log.

## Phase 1 — interface coupons

Print small hook, latch, gap, and registration coupons. Explore latch geometry and release methods separately from decorative styling.

**Exit:** three operators can engage and release the best interface repeatedly without damage.

## Phase 2 — 3 × 3 mechanical panel

Build a complete grid and nine cartridges. Measure alignment, neighbor replacement, retention, moisture paths, and cycle life.

**Exit:** test-plan basics pass and failure modes are documented.

## Phase 3 — appearance and cleanability

Test face materials, textures, grout color, coatings, edge treatments, and cleaning workflow. Keep appearance parts interchangeable with the same carrier interface.

**Exit:** one visual direction and one cleanability approach are selected.

## Phase 4 — installation and substrate study

Evaluate fasteners, shims, wall variation, corners, outlets, termination edges, and removal from representative substrates. Review applicable building and appliance constraints with qualified professionals before real-world claims.

**Exit:** documented installation method and limitations.

## Phase 5 — pilot and manufacturing study

Estimate part count, cycle-time, clip replacement, packaging, tolerances, quality checks, and cost. Compare additive, molded, formed, ceramic, metal, and hybrid paths.

**Exit:** pilot build plan, bill of materials, quality plan, and go/no-go decision.

## Initial backlog

- Create parametric grid, carrier, and clip CAD.
- Build hook/latch fit coupons.
- Choose two prototype materials and document their limits.
- Define a pull-force and cycle-test fixture.
- Create a cleaning and residue inspection method.
- Prototype inside corner and outside termination pieces.
- Test a damaged-tile replacement workflow.
- Research installation constraints and required disclaimers.
- Create a revision-controlled bill of materials.
