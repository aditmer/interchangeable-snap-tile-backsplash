# System architecture

## Layer 1: mounting grid

Use modular grid sections with a nominal 3 × 6 inch tile pitch. The grid contains the visible grout field, wall attachment points, drainage/ventilation paths where appropriate, and the receptacles for the tile hooks and latches.

The grid should be installed level and should tolerate ordinary wall variation through shims or an adjustable mounting interface rather than forcing the decorative tile to compensate.

## Layer 2: tile cartridge

Each cartridge includes a decorative face and a structural carrier. The preferred interaction is:

1. engage two rigid upper hooks;
2. rotate or press the lower edge inward;
3. let a spring clip or flex latch click into the grid;
4. release with a hidden finger recess, suction cup target, or push-release zone.

Magnets may reduce rattle, but should not be the only safety-critical attachment method.

## Interfaces to prototype

- top hook depth and lead-in angle;
- bottom latch deflection and retention force;
- grout reveal and tile-to-tile gap;
- carrier stiffness and face attachment;
- release access without a visible notch collecting soil;
- expansion and warping allowance for the chosen print material.

## Material strategy

Prototype the grid and carrier in a dimensionally stable engineering-grade filament available to the team. Use a non-structural decorative skin only after the mechanical interface is stable. Document each material's temperature, chemical, and moisture limitations from its manufacturer.

## Manufacturing paths

1. FDM prototypes for geometry and cycle tests.
2. Resin, injection-molded, thermoformed, ceramic, metal, or composite faces for later appearance and durability exploration.
3. Replaceable clips as a service part rather than an integral feature that makes the entire cartridge disposable.
